ShadowMux: Off-Screen Lease & Conflict Sentinel

An autonomous agent acquires an atomic SQLite domain lease, mirrors Chrome cookies into an off-screen viewport at (-3200,-3200), pauses cleanly if the human touches the same domain, and stamps an audit receipt.

Themes

Mandatory OS Keychain cookie cloning blocks both newcomers and public-site tasks

Kabir (denied scary macOS Keychain prompt) and Riya (public Greenhouse form) both stalled at sync_cookies (36% of starters blocked).

Steps: sync_cookies

Do: Explain the Keychain prompt before triggering it, and allow ephemeral/unauthenticated shadow profiles when Keychain access is absent or unneeded.

Conflict Sentinel depends on an unverified Chrome extension prerequisite

Siddharth had not installed the Chrome extension, stalling conflict_sentinel_pause when he opened the same domain.

Steps: conflict_sentinel_pause

Do: Check extension presence at setup and fall back to OS window-title / CDP target polling if the extension is missing.

Manual 'Resume' pill gets lost during real multitasking

Naina got pulled into a meeting and never clicked Resume before the lease TTL expired; Meera nearly forgot too.

Steps: arbitrate_handoff

Do: Auto-resume 5–10 seconds after the human switches away from the conflicted domain, and show TTL countdown on the pill.

Gaps in the spec

  • high Keychain denial / Linux keyring failure fallback is missing: Proposal Section 2.4 decrypts Cookies via OS Keychain APIs, but does not define what happens when the user clicks Deny or runs headless Linux.
  • high Paused agent timeout vs resume behavior: When Conflict Sentinel sets PAUSED_FOR_HUMAN, the spec says lease TTL is 120s—if the human takes >120s on their tab, does the stale lock scavenger kill the paused checkpoint?
  • high Multi-monitor negative coordinates collision: If a user arranges an external monitor to the top-left of their primary display in macOS Displays, (-3200, -3200) can land on a visible monitor or snap back on-screen.

How to read this, and its limits

  • These are predictions from a model of your feature, not measurements of real people. Treat every number as a hypothesis to check.
  • The ranges cover random variation and a ±10% wobble on the guessed inputs. They do not cover a wrong model (a missing step, a missing kind of user).
  • Language models asked to play users tend to be more agreeable, more goal-directed and less varied than real people. Qualitative sessions find problems; they do not prove their absence.
  • Rank order (which step is worst, which fix helps most) is more reliable than absolute levels. Calibrate with real data when you have any, and score the run after launch.

Method: each synthetic user gets traits (motivation, skill, patience, trust), a situation and capabilities drawn from their segment, then walks the flow. At each step the chance of carrying on rises with motivation, visible value and trust and falls with effort, unclear screens, errors and fatigue. All formulas are in shoal/sim.py; the model used is saved next to this report as model.json.
